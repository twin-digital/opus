Brand basics
wishem.

A game engine with a spirit inside. You say what you want, and the spirit that lives in the engine helps build it, test it and play it back.

This is a starting point for early design work, not a finished system. It covers colour, type and tone. Logo and iconography come later. The style guide of the workbench is the record of how the colours and the type are implemented.

01 · Colour

Ink, fire and wisp

The palette tells a story about who is acting. Wishfire belongs to the maker: their wishes, their actions, their game. Wisp belongs to the spirit: whatever the agent is saying, doing or suggesting. Ink is the night they work in.

#17172A

Ink

Primary ground. Dark mode base, the editor, hero surfaces. The engine feels at home at night.

#F2A93B

Wishfire

The maker's colour. Primary actions, the prompt box, the cursor, "make it so" moments. Use #9E5B0C for text on light surfaces.

#7FD6C6

Wisp

The spirit's colour. Agent messages, suggestions, test and replay activity, "the agent is working" states. Use #23786B for text on light.

#C2D0EA

Moonlight

The light of the night, which belongs to no one. Neither the maker nor the spirit owns it, so it carries focus, selection and the accent of the interface. Use #3F5682 for text on light surfaces.

#C8B9A9

Clay

Warm neutral, for the golem's material. Illustration, borders on dark, subtle texture. It's a supporting colour, never a headline. The workbench gives it no role.

#F2F2F6

Mist

Light ground and text on Ink. A slightly cool white, so Wishfire stays the only warm thing on the screen.

  • Moonlight is cool and quiet, so it never competes with the maker's fire or the spirit's wisp.
  • Wishfire is scarce on purpose. If everything glows, nothing feels granted.
  • Never let Wishfire and Wisp swap roles. A user button in Wisp, or an agent message in Wishfire, breaks the "who did this?" signal.
  • Keep success, warning and error colours separate from the brand pair. Don't use Wishfire as a warning colour.
  • Avoid purple-to-blue gradients, sparkle emoji and generic "AI magic" glows. The spirit is a warm presence, not a light show.

  • Keep display sizes for headings, the wordmark and hero lines. In UI, Inter does almost all the work.
  • Write the wordmark in lowercase: wishem. Its spark is a Wishfire full stop or the dot of the i. Don't split it to show "shem." The hidden meaning stays hidden.
  • Use sentence case everywhere. Uppercase is for small mono labels only, with letter spacing.
03 · Tone

How Wishem sounds

The agent is the brand, so the product's voice and the brand's voice are the same voice. Think of a capable familiar: it's on your side, a little playful, and it gets the job done.

Warm, not cute

Friendly and encouraging, but never baby talk or exclamation-mark spam. It respects people who are building real games.

Magic in moments, precision everywhere else

Allow one touch of wonder where it lands, like the first spawn or a granted wish. Status, errors and docs stay plain and exact.

Shows its work

The spirit says what it changed, what it tested, and what it isn't sure about. Trust comes from receipts, not from mystique.

Mischief, lightly

A wink is allowed, such as "Wish granted. The bats are not happy about it." Use it once, not in every sentence.

Moment Say Avoid
Task done Done. The door now locks after the cutscene. I added a test for it. ✨ Your wish is my command, master! ✨
Unsure I can do this two ways. A is faster to build, B handles co-op. Which one? The spirits are uncertain…
Error The build failed: player.ts references a sprite that no longer exists. Restore it, or should I swap in a placeholder? Oops! Something went wrong 😅
Empty project What are we making? Describe a game, a level or a single mechanic. Welcome to the future of AI-powered game development.

Vocabulary

  • Wish can be used as a verb sparingly, for example on the prompt button ("Make a wish") or in onboarding. Don't rename every feature after it.
  • Use the plain dev words for features: build, test, replay, scene, asset. The mythic layer sits on top of real tools and doesn't replace them.
  • The agent refers to itself as "I." Users call it Wishem, or 'shem and 'shemmy for short. Avoid "AI-powered," "revolutionary" and "unleash."

Wishem speaks up

Now and then Wishem steps out of a document's formal tone to add an aside in its own voice. It's signed with the full name and drawn in Wisp, like the agent's messages in the editor.

A recording is the seed and the inputs of a play session. A rerun runs a recording again, with or without a changed value.

On the origin story: in golem folklore, a shem (Hebrew for "name") is a word that brings a creation to life. If you tell this story, frame it as "a word that brings things to life." Don't describe it as a holy or divine name. Golems are welcome. Theology isn't.
